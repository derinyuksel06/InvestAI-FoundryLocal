Compound Interest

Source: Investor.gov — U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission

Compound interest is interest earned on both the original principal and previously accumulated interest.

This means that interest can begin earning additional interest over time.

Compounding can cause money to grow faster over longer periods compared with earning interest only on the original principal.

The amount produced by compounding depends on factors such as the starting amount, interest rate, and length of time.

Time can have a significant effect on compound growth because previous gains can contribute to future growth.

Compound interest is an important concept for understanding long-term saving and investing.
