Bonds

Source: Investor.gov — U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission

A bond is a debt security, similar to an IOU.

When an investor buys a bond, the investor is lending money to the bond issuer.

Governments, municipalities, and companies may issue bonds to raise money.

The issuer generally promises to pay interest to the bondholder and repay the principal when the bond reaches maturity.

Corporate bonds are issued by companies. Investors who buy corporate bonds are lending money to the company.

Bonds can involve different types of risk, including the risk that the issuer may fail to make required payments.

Bonds and stocks represent different relationships with a company. A stock represents ownership, while a corporate bond represents money lent to the company.
