SOURCE: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission - How to Read a 10-K

A Form 10-K is an annual report filed by most U.S. public companies with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

A 10-K provides detailed information about a company's business, major risks, and operating and financial results.

The Business section explains what the company does and how its operations work.

The Risk Factors section describes important risks that could affect the company.

The financial statements provide information about the company's financial performance and financial position.

Management's Discussion and Analysis, often called MD&A, explains management's view of the company's results, trends, and factors affecting performance.

Investors can use a 10-K to research a company, but the report should be analyzed critically rather than treating every statement as a prediction of future performance.
